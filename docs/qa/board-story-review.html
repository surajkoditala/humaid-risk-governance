Summary

The headline findings before the detail.

The backlog itself is in good shape

All 73 stories have acceptance criteria — not one is empty. They average five criteria each, they are written in Given/When/Then, and the platform stories (Epics 15–18) are unusually specific for infrastructure work. This is a better-specified backlog than most delivery teams have, and it is the main reason a review like this is possible at all.

The five things that matter most

  1. US-9.2 (Guarantee immutability) — the flagship governance claim, implemented by a database trigger, and tested by nothing at all. No HTTP endpoint can reach it.
  2. US-12.1 (Apply schema to a deployed environment) — still New, still unowned, and now blocking a live deployment attempt.

How each story was rated

Two independent axes. A story can be fully built and untested, or well specified and unbuilt — conflating them hides exactly the problems worth finding.

AxisValuesJudged by
BuiltYes Partial NoReading the implementing code, schema or pipeline — never the story's state field
Tested●●● strong · ●●○ partial · ●○○ little or noneAn executed test that names the story or covers its criteria

●●● means covered at more than one layer, including one that cannot be bypassed. ●●○ means one layer only, or a known gap inside the story. ●○○ means no automated coverage that would catch a regression.

One deliberate rule

The story's state field was ignored when judging "Built". Two stories are marked Done whose work is incomplete, and many marked New are fully implemented — the board's state reflects workflow bookkeeping, not delivery. Reading the code is the only reliable method.

Cross-cutting findings

Patterns that only appear when you read all 73 stories together.

1 · The override rule is the least-tested rule in the product

"A human can override any AI output; must state a reason; both original and override are retained" is a non-negotiable constraint in the project brief and appears in US-2.2, US-4.2, US-6.1, US-7.2 and US-10.1. Of those five, only US-7.2 has a test that a blank reason is refused.

The implementation looks right everywhere — every service method takes a reason and the stored functions write the audit row in the same transaction. But "looks right" is not "proven", and this is the rule the 15% human-in-the-loop criterion rests on. Five API tests would close it.

2 · Data-layer guarantees are systematically untested

Four separate stories depend on something the database enforces: audit immutability (US-9.2), residual risk above zero (US-7.1), snapshot immutability (US-14.4), and config versioning (US-10.1). None of the four has a test that reaches the database. One Testcontainers suite closes all of them at once — which is why it has been the top recommendation since 12 September.

3 · The platform epics are better specified than the product epics

Epics 15–18 average 6.6 criteria per story; Epics 1–10 average 3.2. The infrastructure stories are also more precise — they name resources, settings and expected outputs. That is unusual and worth keeping: it is why this review could assess them against the Terraform directly.

4 · Notifications appear in three stories and are implemented in none

US-1.3, US-8.1 and US-8.3 each require the Product Owner to be notified. There is no notification mechanism in the codebase. This is a coherent scope decision if made deliberately — but it is currently an implicit one, and three stories cannot be marked complete until it is either built or the criteria are removed with a stated reason.

5 · Board state does not reflect delivery

Seventy-one of 73 stories are New, including US-6.3 and US-8.2 which are among the best-implemented and best-tested on the board. The two marked Done are the two whose work is not complete. The state field is currently the least reliable signal on the board — worth a five-minute pass before any demo or review.
