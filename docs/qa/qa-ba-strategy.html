01

The problem statement

What we were given, what was deliberately left out, and what we added.

The brief is incomplete on purpose

Myridius issued this problem statement deliberately under-specified, and expanding it is itself graded — 10% of the total score is "context engineering & requirement expansion". So the gaps below are not oversights in the brief; filling them is the assignment. That reframes the BA role on this project from capturing requirements to deriving and defending them.

The client and the function

A large national bank, roughly $500B in assets, operating consumer banking, commercial banking, payments and wealth management — and heavily supervised on financial crime risk controls. The relevant function is Financial Crimes Risk Management (FCRM).

The business problem

A national bank (~$500B assets) must assess financial-crime risk before any business change goes live — a new product, a feature, a process change, a vendor, a new geography, or a new customer segment. Today that assessment runs on email, Word, Excel and SharePoint. It takes 15–20 business days, two analysts can reach different conclusions on the same change, and reconstructing the reasoning behind a past rating for an examiner is slow and incomplete.

The Risk Assessment Workbench carries a change request through intake → assessment → committee decision on one governed platform, with an audit trail an examiner can read.

Actors

ActorDoesMust never be able to
Product OwnerRaises the change request, attaches documents, tracks statusSee analyst scoring rationale; act on the assessment itself
FCRM AnalystReviews AI output, overrides with reason, scores, finalizes, owns configurationRoute an unreviewed assessment to committee
Risk CommitteeReviews the finalized assessment and votesVote anonymously or have a vote aggregated away

What triggers an assessment

Six change types, and the type drives everything downstream — which fields intake demands, which risk categories get proposed, and which controls apply. This enumeration is closed, enforced by a database CHECK constraint.

Change typeExamplePrimary FFIEC category
ProductLaunch same-day international wire transfersProducts & Services + Customers (secondary)
FeatureRaise the daily transfer limitProducts & Services
ProcessChange how customer tickets are routedDelivery Channels
VendorOnboard a third-party KYC verification vendorCustomers & Entities + Delivery Channels
GeographyOpen retail branches in a FATF-monitored jurisdictionGeographic Locations (heaviest)
Customer SegmentBegin onboarding money services businessesCustomers & Entities (heaviest)

The three constraints everything hangs off

  1. The system prepares, humans decide. Nothing is auto-approved or auto-rejected. Every AI touchpoint pairs with a human review gate in code.
  2. Controls mitigate, never eliminate. Residual risk can never reach zero — enforced at calculation time and at configuration time.
  3. Grounded in a citable framework. Risk decomposition uses the FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual's four categories. Nothing invented, every category carries a citation.
Why this matters for test design

These three are not features — they are invariants. A test suite for this product is judged on whether it can prove them, not on line coverage. That is the organising principle for everything in sections 4 and 5.

What the brief did not say

Five things a real FCRM engagement would have specified and this brief left open. Each one changes the design, so each is a BA decision that must be stated rather than allowed to happen by accident.

  1. What visibility Product Owners have into analyst reasoning and scoring.
  2. Whether the risk framework is fixed or configurable per jurisdiction and business line.
  3. Whether a scoring override needs secondary sign-off before finalization.
  4. Whether committee decisioning is majority, unanimous, or chair-decided.
  5. Who may approve scoring and workflow configuration changes.

Their current status is tracked in §02.

03

The solution

How a change request actually moves through the platform, and which parts of that journey are deterministic by choice.

System architecture

A 5-layer Clean Architecture .NET solution using Dapper against named PostgreSQL stored functions — never an ORM, never ad-hoc SQL. Mock External Systems is deliberately a separate service, standing in for the CRM / Core Banking / Vendor Management systems a real bank already runs.

The journey of one change request

Nine steps from submission to decision. This is the sequence the three UI journey tests will follow, and the sequence an examiner would reconstruct from the audit trail.

#StepWhoWhat the system does
1SubmitProduct OwnerAssigns an immutable CR-2026-00001 number; type-specific fields stored as JSON; status Submitted
2IngestSystemLinks the request to a Mock Systems customer/product/vendor and freezes an immutable snapshot — everything downstream reads the snapshot, never the live system
3Attach documentsProduct OwnerPDF/DOCX/XLSX only, enforced by a DB CHECK. Replacing a document creates a new version row, never an overwrite
4Map categories AISystem → AnalystProposes FFIEC categories from a fixed allowed list; analyst adds/removes with a mandatory reason
5Surface policySystem → AnalystPostgres full-text search ranks policy chunks; analyst marks each relied upon or not relevant
6Extract fields AISystem → AnalystStructured facts with a source excerpt per field; low-confidence pre-flagged needsReview
7Draft narrative AISystem → AnalystOne narrative per category, citing only supplied sources; unsupported claims returned separately, never folded into the text
8Score & finalizeAnalystDeterministic scoring; FinalizeAsync blocks until every category has a reviewed narrative and a policy decision
9Vote & feed backCommitteeIndividual votes recorded; on quorum the decision resolves and pushes a go-live flag back to Mock Systems

The scoring model

Entirely deterministic — no model anywhere in this path. The formula is one line of PL/pgSQL:

residual = inherent_rating − (control_effectiveness × max_mitigation_factor)

Residual risk cannot reach zero, and that is a matter of arithmetic rather than of anyone remembering to check. The bounds enforce it:

TermBoundEnforced by
inherent_rating1 – 5CHECK (inherent_rating >= 1 AND <= 5)
control_effectiveness0 – 1CHECK (>= 0 AND <= 1)
max_mitigation_factor0 – 0.999…CHECK (>= 0 AND < 1.0) — strictly below 1
residual_rating> 0CHECK (residual_rating > 0) — belt and braces

Worst case with the seeded default of 0.85: 1 − (1 × 0.85) = 0.15. The floor holds at four independent layers — the arithmetic, the DB constraints, the stored function, and a C# guard in ScoringService. A configuration change is a new version row, so an assessment keeps scoring against the config that was active when it started.

Where AI is used — and deliberately is not

This table is worth defending out loud: 5% of the score is explicitly for judgement about when not to use an LLM.

EpicApproachReasoning
2 — Category mappingAIJudgement over free text, grounded against a fixed list so a category cannot be invented
3 — Policy researchDeterministicRanking indexed text by relevance is solved search. An LLM adds latency, cost and nondeterminism for no accuracy gain
4 — Document extractionAIPulling structure from free text is exactly the task LLMs are good at; every field carries a confidence and a source excerpt
5 — Narrative draftingAIGenuinely generative — but permitted to cite only what it was handed
7 — ScoringDeterministicA fixed formula with a provable guarantee. An LLM here would trade a proof for a probability

How fabrication is prevented

Each of the three AI clients drops or flags anything it cannot trace back to its input, rather than passing it through:

  • Category mapping — discards any proposed category whose id is not in the supplied allowed list. The model physically cannot introduce a category.
  • Document extraction — requires a sourceExcerpt per field, and forces needsReview on anything the model itself flagged low-confidence.
  • Narrative drafting — unsupported statements must be returned in a separate unsupportedClaims array, never folded into the narrative text.
An honest limit

The first guard is structural — the code filters the list, so it holds regardless of what the model returns. The second and third are contractual: they depend on the model populating the right JSON field. If it fabricates inside narrativeText instead, only the analyst review gate catches it. The eval suite's own README states this plainly rather than claiming coverage it does not have, which is the right call.

Working method

  • Acceptance criteria are the spec. Every test I write names the US/AC it proves, in the test's own doc comment. That is what makes the traceability matrix mechanical to produce rather than a separate invention.
  • Ambiguity becomes a question, not an assumption. Q3 and Q5 go to the team at standup; they change the design if answered, so they cannot sit in a document unread.
  • Findings carry reproductions. A defect raised with a failing test is acted on; a defect raised as a doc bullet is discussed. §7 follows that rule.
06

Testing approach

Risk-based, invariant-led, and deliberately not chasing a coverage percentage.

Prioritisation

With 18 days and one person, test effort follows two things: the judging weights, and what breaks the product's own claims. Those happen to agree.

PriorityWhatServes
1Governance invariants — finalize gate, audit immutability, residual > 0, individual votes15% human-in-the-loop
2CI running tests + evals at all20% SDLC automation
3Adversarial AI eval cases10% evaluation framework
4Authorization / RBAC5% production readiness
5Three UI journeysdemo evidence

Rules we hold ourselves to

  • A test that cannot fail is not a test. Every governance test is verified by mutation — break the rule deliberately, confirm the test catches it, revert. Done for the finalize gate already (§6).
  • Known gaps are recorded, not hidden. Where the product does not yet do the right thing, the test asserts the right behaviour and is marked expected-to-fail, so the fix announces itself. It is never deleted or weakened to keep the board green.
  • Characterization over silence. Where current behaviour is arguably wrong but changing it is the dev's call, we pin it with a clearly-commented test so any future change is deliberate.
  • Never let a dead environment look like a pass. Expected-to-fail tests pass when they fail for any reason, including a refused connection — so the suite aborts up front if the API is unreachable.
  • Scope claims honestly. If a probe used a non-existent id, the report says so and says what it therefore does not prove.

Defect workflow

  1. Reproduce with a command or a test — no defect without one.
  2. Classify severity by what it breaks in the product's own claims, not by crash loudness.
  3. Land the failing/characterization test on the QA branch first.
  4. Raise at standup with the repro and the owning user story.
  5. When fixed, the marker flips and tells us — remove the annotation, not the test.

Entry and exit criteria

GateCriteria
Entry to testStack starts clean from a fresh clone; schema + seed apply; /api/Ping returns 200; the user story has acceptance criteria.
Exit for submissionAll four governance invariants proven by a test that has been shown to fail when broken; CI runs unit + API suites on every PR; all 12 eval cases exercised against a real model; every open defect either fixed or documented with a repro.

Engineering judgement — where we deliberately do not automate

The UI suite stays at three journeys, not thirty. UI tests are the most expensive evidence per hour in this repo — they need the Vite dev server plus both .NET services plus Postgres — and a broad UI suite would mostly re-prove what 74 unit tests already cover far more cheaply. Three role journeys (PO submits → Analyst assesses and finalizes → Committee votes) are the product demo and exercise the whole governance chain. That is the right amount.

The CI gap — priority one

Two Azure DevOps pipelines exist and both are path-filtered to iac/environments/dev/*. Nothing in CI ever runs dotnet test or the eval suite. On the branches page, the application branches show a blank check-status column while every IaC branch shows a green tick — that is the gap made visible.

DEF-003MediumRobustness

An unknown id surfaces as HTTP 500

A well-formed but non-existent id reaches Postgres and the resulting exception escapes as a 500 rather than a 4xx. BaseApiController.ExecuteAsync catches and logs it, but its fallback is OperationResult.Failure → 500 — so "you sent an id that does not exist" reads to the caller as "the server broke". OperationResult already has NotFound and BadRequest shapes built for exactly this.

DEF-004MediumGovernance

Reachable two ways: the AI proposes nothing — which is US-2.1 AC3's legitimate "no framework mapping configured" path, correctly returning empty rather than guessing — or an analyst deactivates every proposed category.

DEF-005MediumData / completeness

The control catalogue is empty — scoring has nothing to credit

The control table is created by the schema but never populated by any seed file. US-7.1 AC3 requires that an analyst "can trace which specific controls were credited and each control's configured mitigation weight" — with an empty catalogue there is nothing to credit, nothing to trace, and control_ids_credited is permanently [].

Scoring still runs, because control_effectiveness is passed in as a number rather than derived from the credited controls. So the arithmetic works while the explainability requirement behind it does not — which is precisely the kind of gap a coverage metric would never surface.

How to showcase this to the team

A five-minute live demo, in this order. It builds from the most unambiguous finding to the most nuanced, which is also least-to-most likely to attract debate.
