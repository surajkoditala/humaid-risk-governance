Risk Assessment Workbench

One change request, start to finish

Every step a Product Owner's request takes on its way to a committee decision — where AI helps, where it's deliberately kept out, and where a human always has the final say.

Presentation opener~30-40 sec, before the live demo

  1. ProblemAt a $500B bank, every new product, feature, or vendor has to clear a financial-crime risk review before it can launch. Today that runs on email, Word docs, and spreadsheets — takes 15-20 business days, and two analysts can reach different conclusions on the same request.
  2. SolutionWe built the Risk Assessment Workbench — one governed platform that carries a change request from intake to committee decision. AI helps categorize risk, research policy, extract documents, and draft the assessment — but a human always reviews, and the committee always decides. Nothing is ever auto-approved.
  3. What you'll seeI'll walk one real request — launching a prepaid settlement card — end to end: intake, AI risk categorization grounded in the actual FFIEC BSA/AML manual, policy research, document extraction, AI-drafted narrative, risk scoring that can never hit zero, and a committee vote — with a full audit trail behind every step.

1
IntakeNo AI

Product Owner submits a change type, title, description, and an optional document (PDF, DOCX, XLSX). For a product, customer segment, or vendor change, they also link the specific Mock Systems record — an immutable snapshot of its risk context is captured for AI grounding later.

Product Owner

2
CategorizeAI proposes

AI proposes FFIEC risk categories — but only from a fixed allow-list for that specific change type (e.g. a Product change can only get Products & Services or Customers & Entities proposed). Never invented outside that list. The analyst accepts, adds, or removes any category, always with a reason.

Analyst reviews

3
Research policyNo AI — search

Analyst searches the FFIEC BSA/AML policy library and marks which sections were relied on. Plain text search, not a model call — policy lookup doesn't need one.

Analyst

4
Extract fieldsAI extracts

If a document was attached, its text is parsed automatically at upload (deterministic, not AI). AI then reads that text and pulls out structured facts (e.g. vendor name, data access scope) — every value tagged AI-extracted until an analyst corrects it.

Analyst corrects

5
Draft narrativeAI drafts

AI writes the assessment write-up for each mapped category, citing the relied-on policy and the facts extracted from the document. It stays marked "AI-drafted" until the analyst edits, regenerates, or explicitly accepts it.

Analyst reviews

6
Score the riskHuman input

Analyst rates inherent risk and control effectiveness. The system then calculates residual risk — a formula built so it can never reach zero, because controls reduce risk, they don't erase it.

Analyst

7
FinalizeGate

Every mapped category must have a reviewed narrative, at least one policy-reliance decision, and a calculated score — before Finalize unlocks. Once finalized, the assessment locks; no more edits.

Analyst

8
Route to committeeNo AI

A separate, explicit action from Finalize. Until the analyst does this, a finalized assessment is locked but still not visible in the committee's queue.

Analyst

9
Committee voteHuman decides

Each committee member votes independently — Approve, Reject, Defer, or Approve with conditions, reasons required for anything but a plain approve. Once the configured quorum is reached, the system resolves the outcome automatically (reject and defer take priority over approve).

Committee

10
Decision & audit trailPermanent record

The Product Owner sees the outcome. Every AI output, every human edit or override, every vote — logged permanently and never editable, so the reasoning behind any decision can be reconstructed later.

System

The rule that holds the whole thing together: AI proposes, extracts, and drafts — a person always reviews, and the committee always decides. Nothing in this system is ever auto-approved or auto-rejected.
