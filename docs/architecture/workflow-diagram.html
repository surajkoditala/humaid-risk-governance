Risk Assessment Workbench

One change request, start to finish

Every step a request takes, from someone proposing a change to a committee deciding on it — where AI helps, where it's deliberately kept out, and where a human always has the final say.

Presentation opener~30-40 sec, before the live demo

  1. ProblemAt a $500B bank, every new product, feature, or vendor has to clear a financial-crime risk review before it can launch. Today that runs on email, Word docs, and spreadsheets — takes 15-20 business days, and two analysts can reach different conclusions on the same request.
  2. SolutionWe built the Risk Assessment Workbench — one platform that carries a request from first submission to a committee decision. AI helps categorize risk, pull facts from documents, and draft the write-up — but a human always reviews its work, and the committee always makes the final call. Nothing is ever auto-approved.
  3. What you'll seeI'll walk one real request — launching a prepaid settlement card — end to end: intake, AI risk categorization grounded in the actual FFIEC BSA/AML manual, policy research, document extraction, AI-drafted narrative, risk scoring that can never hit zero, and a committee vote — with a full audit trail behind every step.
  4. Transition"Let's jump in." → switch straight to the Intake / Categories tab.

1
Submit a requestNo AI

A Product Owner describes what they want to launch — what kind of change it is, what it involves, and any supporting document. If it touches a specific product, customer group, or vendor, they link that record too, so there's full context from the start.

Product Owner

2
Categorize the riskAI suggests

AI suggests which risk categories apply, based on official banking-regulator guidance — it only picks from categories that make sense for this kind of change, never invents new ones. A human reviews and can override: accept the suggestion, add a category, or remove one — always stating why.

Analyst reviews

3
Find supporting policyNo AI — search

An analyst searches the bank's policy library for relevant guidance and marks which parts support the assessment. This is a plain keyword search, not AI — looking up a document doesn't need one.

Analyst

4
Pull facts from the documentAI extracts

AI reads the attached document and pulls out key facts — like a vendor's name or what customer data it touches. Every fact is clearly labeled as AI-generated. A human checks and can correct any value before it's used.

Analyst corrects

5
Draft the write-upAI drafts

AI writes a first draft of the risk write-up for each category, using the policy and facts gathered so far. It stays labeled "AI-drafted" until a human edits it, asks for a new draft, or approves it as-is.

Analyst reviews

6
Score the riskHuman input

An analyst rates how risky the change is on its own, and how well existing safeguards reduce that risk. The system then works out the risk that's left over — built so that number can never hit zero, because no safeguard removes risk completely.

Analyst

7
FinalizeGate

Before this can be finalized, every category needs a reviewed write-up, supporting policy, and a score. Once finalized, the assessment is locked — no more edits.

Analyst

8
Send to committeeNo AI

A separate step from finalizing — the analyst sends the assessment on for a decision. Until then, it stays out of the committee's queue, even once it's finalized.

Analyst

9
Committee decidesHuman decides

Each committee member votes independently — Approve, Reject, Defer, or Approve with Conditions — giving a reason for anything other than a plain approve. Once enough members have voted, the system works out the final outcome (a Reject or Defer always outweighs an Approve).

Committee

10
Outcome & full historyPermanent record

The Product Owner is told the outcome. Every AI suggestion, every human edit, and every vote is permanently recorded and can never be changed — so anyone can trace exactly how and why a decision was reached.

System

The rule that holds the whole thing together: AI can suggest, extract, and draft — but a human always reviews and can override, and the committee always makes the final call. Nothing is ever auto-approved or auto-rejected.
